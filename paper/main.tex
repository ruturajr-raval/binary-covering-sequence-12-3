\begin{abstract}
An \((n,R)\) binary covering sequence is a positive-length cyclic binary
sequence whose length-\(n\) windows cover every binary word of length \(n\)
within Hamming distance \(R\). The published finite frontier for the
parameter \((12,3)\) was reduced in 2025 to
\(34\leq L(12,3)\leq 36\) by combining a table lower bound with an explicit
36-bit construction. We determine the exact value. Two independently written
exhaustive programs enumerate every binary sequence of every positive length
from 1 through 35. Rotation, reversal, and bit complement reduce
\(68{,}719{,}476{,}734\) raw sequences to \(506{,}526{,}514\)
representatives. The C++ and Rust traversals find no covering representative,
and every per-length representative count agrees with independently
implemented Burnside calculations. Three verifier implementations confirm
that the attributed 36-bit construction has covering radius exactly 3.
Therefore \(L(12,3)=36\). Complete source, logs, metadata, hashes, and replay
tools accompany the result.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Covering sequences are approximate de Bruijn sequences. Instead of requiring
every length-\(n\) word to occur exactly, one requires every target word to be
close in Hamming distance to at least one cyclic window. Chung and Cooper
introduced this problem and established general bounds
\cite{chung-cooper}. Chee, Etzion, Ta, and Vu later developed explicit
constructions and compiled finite lower and upper bounds
\cite{chee-etzion-ta-vu}. Their 2025 table records
\[
  34\leq L(12,3)\leq 40.
\]
Rosin subsequently reported a public length-36 construction
\cite{rosin}, reducing the working frontier to
\[
  34\leq L(12,3)\leq 36.
\]

This paper closes the remaining two candidate lengths by complete
enumeration. The computation is small enough to repeat on a multicore
workstation but large enough that direct inspection is impossible. The
release therefore uses two separately written exhaustive implementations,
independent symmetry-count calculations, exact full-target coverage, retained
logs, and source-bound metadata.

Two later papers develop general and asymptotic covering-sequence
constructions \cite{etzion-2026,ta-vu-2026}. The public-source audit dated
September 7, 2026 located no earlier public exact determination of this
finite cell. That audit supports the release context but is not part of the
mathematical argument.

\section{Definitions and statement}

\begin{definition}
Let \(x=x_0x_1\cdots x_{L-1}\) be a positive-length cyclic binary sequence.
For \(0\leq i<L\), its length-12 window at phase \(i\) is
\[
  W_i(x)=x_i x_{i+1}\cdots x_{i+11},
\]
where every subscript is reduced modulo \(L\). This convention also applies
when \(L<12\), so positions may repeat inside a window.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
The sequence \(x\) is a binary \((12,3)\) covering sequence when every
\(y\in\{0,1\}^{12}\) satisfies
\[
  \min_{0\leq i<L} d_H(y,W_i(x))\leq 3.
\]
The minimum possible positive length is denoted by \(L(12,3)\).
\end{definition}

Our main result is the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
\[
  L(12,3)=36.
\]
\end{theorem}

\section{Symmetry reduction}

Let \(D_L\) be the dihedral group generated by cyclic rotation and reversal
of \(L\) positions. Let \(C_2\) act by complementing every bit. These actions
commute, so the abstract group \(D_L\times C_2\), of order \(4L\), acts on
the sequences. For \(L=1\) or \(L=2\), this action is nonfaithful.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:invariance}
The covering property is invariant under rotation, reversal, and bit
complement.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Rotation only changes the starting phase of the same cyclic windows.
Reversal replaces every window by its reversal; reversal is an isometry of
the binary Hamming cube and permutes all target words. Complementation also
is a Hamming-cube isometry and permutes all target words. Hence each
operation preserves whether the union of radius-3 balls around the windows
is all of \(\{0,1\}^{12}\).
\end{proof}

Each exhaustive program retains a bit string only when no transformed string
is smaller under its stated integer or lexicographic convention. Every orbit
has a minimum, so this rule retains at least one representative and at most
one distinct minimum.

As an independent completeness check, the expected orbit count is calculated
with Burnside's lemma. Define
\[
  A_L=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}2^{\gcd(L,k)}
\]
for ordinary rotations. A complemented rotation by \(k\) fixes a string only
when every cycle of that rotation has even length, giving
\[
  B_L=
  \sum_{\substack{0\leq k<L\\
  L/\gcd(L,k)\ {\rm even}}}
  2^{\gcd(L,k)}.
\]
For ordinary reflections, the fixed-string contribution is
\[
  F_L=
  \begin{cases}
    L\,2^{(L+1)/2}, & L\ {\rm odd},\\[3pt]
    \dfrac{L}{2}\left(2^{L/2+1}+2^{L/2}\right),
      & L\ {\rm even}.
  \end{cases}
\]
A complemented reflection can fix a string only when the reflection has no
fixed position. Thus
\[
  \overline{F}_L=
  \begin{cases}
    0, & L\ {\rm odd},\\[3pt]
    \dfrac{L}{2}2^{L/2}, & L\ {\rm even}.
  \end{cases}
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:burnside}
The number \(N_L\) of binary length-\(L\) sequences up to rotation,
reversal, and complement is
\[
  N_L=\frac{A_L+B_L+F_L+\overline{F}_L}{4L}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a rotation by \(k\), the position permutation has
\(\gcd(L,k)\) cycles, producing \(2^{\gcd(L,k)}\) fixed strings. A
complemented cycle admits two assignments exactly when its length is even.
The reflection contributions follow from the standard cycle structures:
one fixed position for odd \(L\), either two or zero fixed positions for even
\(L\), and transpositions elsewhere. Burnside's lemma then averages the
fixed-string counts over all \(4L\) transformations.
\end{proof}

The C++ and Rust evidence checkers implement this count independently. Both
predictions match both enumerators at every length from 1 through 35.

\section{Exact coverage test}

The ambient space has \(2^{12}=4096\) targets. For each 12-bit center
\(c\), both implementations precompute the radius-3 Hamming ball
\[
  B_3(c)=\{y\in\{0,1\}^{12}:d_H(c,y)\leq 3\}.
\]
Its size is
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{3}\binom{12}{j}=299.
\]
Each ball is stored as a 4096-bit vector. For a candidate sequence \(x\), the
covered-target vector is
\[
  C(x)=\bigvee_{i=0}^{L-1} B_3(W_i(x)).
\]
The candidate covers exactly when every bit of \(C(x)\) is one.

The cyclic windows are generated directly from modulo-\(L\) bit positions.
No assumption \(L\geq 12\) is made. Duplicate windows do not affect the
bitwise union.

\section{Exhaustive algorithms}

For every \(L=1,\ldots,35\), the programs traverse all integers from zero
through \(2^L-1\). Each integer is interpreted as a binary cyclic sequence.
The high-level computation is:

\begin{verbatim}
for L = 1,...,35:
    for every binary sequence x of length L:
        if x is not the minimum in its symmetry orbit:
            continue
        increment representative count
        form every cyclic length-12 window of x
        union the radius-3 balls of those windows
        if every target is covered:
            report a cover
\end{verbatim}

The total raw count is
\[
  \sum_{L=1}^{35}2^L=2^{36}-2
  =68{,}719{,}476{,}734.
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:completeness}
If the exhaustive traversal reports no covering representative for a fixed
length \(L\), then no binary \((12,3)\) covering sequence of length \(L\)
exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every raw sequence belongs to a symmetry orbit. The canonicality test retains
the minimum of every orbit, and Proposition~\ref{prop:burnside} independently
checks the exact number of retained orbits. By Lemma~\ref{lem:invariance}, all
members of an orbit have the same covering status. Therefore rejecting every
retained representative rejects every raw sequence.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent implementations}

The primary implementation is C++20. It partitions the raw integer interval
among worker threads, rejects noncanonical strings, constructs cyclic windows,
and unions precomputed Hamming-ball bitsets. It records per-length raw counts,
representative counts, deterministic aggregate checksums, the smallest
best-uncovered representative under its internal encoding, and elapsed time.

The second implementation is written in Rust without external crate
dependencies. It has separate sequence parsing, transformation, canonicality,
window, coverage, partitioning, and JSON-output code. Its tests compare the
optimized bitset coverage routine with a direct target-by-target Hamming
oracle, compare optimized canonicality with complete orbit minimization,
check small Burnside counts, verify serial/parallel agreement, and validate
the known length-36 witness.

The evidence validators also use separate Burnside implementations. The C++
checker derives cycle structures by explicit permutation traversal. The Rust
checker uses closed reflection formulas and rotation cycle counts.

\section{The length-36 witness}

The public CPro1 witness, retained in normalized form in the accompanying
repository, is
\[
\begin{split}
  x={}&010100011011000110111110101110010000.
\end{split}
\]
Direct enumeration reports:
\[
\begin{array}{lr}
\text{sequence length} & 36\\
\text{cyclic windows} & 36\\
\text{distinct cyclic windows} & 36\\
\text{ambient targets} & 4096\\
\text{covering radius} & 3\\
\text{uncovered targets at radius 3} & 0.
\end{array}
\]
Python, Rust, and C++ verifiers agree on validity. The witness is attributed
prior art and is not claimed as an original construction.

\section{Proof of the main theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:completeness} and the two complete traversals, no
binary \((12,3)\) covering sequence has positive length at most 35. Hence
\[
  L(12,3)\geq 36.
\]
The attributed 36-bit witness covers all 4096 targets within radius 3, so
\[
  L(12,3)\leq 36.
\]
Therefore \(L(12,3)=36\).
\end{proof}

\section{Limitations}

This is a reproducible computer-assisted theorem, not a formally verified
proof. The source and computation remain part of the trusted base. The full
logs record aggregate results rather than every representative, because a
complete representative list would be impractically large. Independent
implementations, independently counted symmetry orbits, exact target
coverage, source-bound metadata, sanitizer checks, and complete reruns reduce
but do not eliminate implementation risk.

No external mathematical or peer review is claimed. The public-source audit
cannot exclude unpublished, private, deleted, or unindexed work. No
classification or count of all optimal length-36 sequences is claimed.

\section{Future work}

The most direct strengthening would formalize the symmetry action, Burnside
count, cyclic-window semantics, and Hamming-ball kernel in a proof assistant.
Another direction is a compact independently checkable exclusion certificate,
for example a proof-producing SAT partition or decision diagram. The same
enumeration architecture can also be applied to neighboring finite
covering-sequence cells. Finally, the length-34 and length-35 nearcovers may
contain structural information useful beyond this parameter.
